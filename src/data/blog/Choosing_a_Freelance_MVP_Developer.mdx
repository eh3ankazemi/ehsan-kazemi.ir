An MVP, or minimum viable product, is a small but usable version of a product that helps you test a business assumption with real users. The goal is not to build every feature on your wish list. It is to learn something valuable with the smallest reasonable scope.

## Define the scope before hiring

Explain the problem your product solves and who its primary users are. Then divide potential features into two groups: what is essential to test the idea and what can wait for a later release. This prioritization helps prevent the project from expanding without a clear reason.

For example, an initial booking product might only need account creation, available time slots, and booking submission. Advanced reports, a separate mobile app, or complex automation could come later.

## How much does an MVP cost?

Cost depends on complexity, design, integrations, and quality requirements. A simple MVP with a limited feature set may cost around **$5,000–$15,000**. A full-stack product with authentication, dashboards, payments, or AI features could cost **$15,000–$50,000 or more**. These ranges are for early planning only and vary by market and project scope.

To compare freelancers quickly, prepare a short project brief and ask each candidate to estimate the same deliverables, timeline, assumptions, and exclusions. An estimate without those details is difficult to compare fairly.

## Questions to ask a developer

1. How does your previous work relate to our product or requirements?
2. How would you break the work into reviewable milestones?
3. What technical assumptions and risks do you see?
4. Will we receive the source code, documentation, and account access?
5. How will testing, deployment, and post-launch fixes be handled?
6. If the project includes AI, how will you evaluate the quality of its outputs?

The right developer does more than write code. They should be able to discuss feature prioritization, user experience, and how to keep the first release focused. They should also explain limitations and risks instead of promising certainty before investigating the problem.

## Set clear expectations

Before work begins, agree on code ownership, schedule, payment milestones, and acceptance criteria for each delivery. Define how scope changes will be handled, since new features can affect both budget and timeline. Staged delivery gives you a chance to review progress and adjust the product direction before spending the full budget.

## Final thoughts

The fastest way to compare MVP freelancers is to give them the same project brief and evaluate the clarity of their proposals—not just their hourly rates. Choose someone who understands the problem, keeps the first release realistic, and is transparent about delivery and ownership.
