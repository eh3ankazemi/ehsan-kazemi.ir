To hire an MVP developer, define the user problem, prioritize only the features needed to test it, and compare candidates using the same project brief. An MVP, or minimum viable product, is a small but usable first release—not a complete version of every idea on your roadmap.

## What should an MVP developer deliver?

Agree on deliverables before work begins. Depending on the project, a functional MVP may include:

- A deployed, usable product with the agreed core user flow
- Responsive interface and essential accessibility behavior
- Backend, database, and integrations required by the agreed scope
- Source code, setup instructions, and environment configuration
- Basic tests, error handling, and handover documentation

Analytics, admin tools, payments, AI functionality, and ongoing support should be explicitly included or excluded. “MVP” does not automatically mean those features are part of the quote.

## How much does it cost to build an MVP?

Cost depends on scope, design, integrations, and quality requirements. As broad international planning ranges, a simple MVP with limited features may cost around **$5,000–$15,000**. A full-stack product with authentication, dashboards, payments, or AI features may cost **$15,000–$50,000 or more**. These are not fixed quotes; local rates and project assumptions can change the total substantially.

The fastest way to compare MVP freelancers is to send the same brief to each candidate and ask for scope, timeline, price, assumptions, and exclusions. Compare what each quote includes, not only the headline number.

## How to compare freelance MVP developers

- **Relevant experience:** Can the developer show work with similar users, workflows, or technical requirements?
- **Clear milestones:** Are the work and review points divided into stages you can approve?
- **Product thinking:** Do they help distinguish essential validation features from nice-to-haves?
- **Technical clarity:** Are assumptions, risks, and third-party dependencies explained?
- **Ownership and handover:** Will you receive the source code, documentation, and account access?
- **Launch and support:** Are testing, deployment, bug fixes, and ongoing support described?

If your MVP needs an AI feature, ask how its quality will be measured and what happens when the system is uncertain. Read [how freelance AI development is scoped and priced](/blog/Freelance_AI_Development_Cost) before comparing AI-specific proposals.

## Questions to ask before you hire

1. What is the smallest release that can test our main assumption?
2. What exactly will be delivered, and what is out of scope?
3. What risks or dependencies could change the schedule or cost?
4. How will we review progress and decide whether a milestone is accepted?
5. Who owns the code, design files, and service accounts?
6. What is included in launch support, and what will cost extra?

## How to turn a software idea into a functional MVP

Write a short brief covering the target user, the problem, the main user journey, must-have features, and how you will measure success. Ask the developer to estimate that scope in milestones. For a web product, [custom full-stack development costs and technology choices](/blog/Custom_Full_Stack_Development_Cost) can help you prepare questions about frontend, backend, and integrations.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the fastest way to compare MVP freelancers?

Give each candidate the same concise brief and compare their proposed scope, deliverables, milestones, risks, and total cost. This makes differences between proposals easier to spot.

### Should I hire a freelancer or an agency for an MVP?

A freelancer may suit a focused project when one person has the required skills. An agency may fit work that needs several specialties and coordinated delivery. Choose based on scope, budget, and how much project management you can provide.

### Does an MVP need to be polished?

It needs to be reliable and usable enough to test the core product assumption. Features and visual refinements that do not support that test can often wait.

## Final thoughts

Choose an MVP developer who understands the user problem, keeps the first release focused, and is transparent about scope and ownership. A clear brief and staged milestones reduce uncertainty and help you learn before investing in a larger product.
