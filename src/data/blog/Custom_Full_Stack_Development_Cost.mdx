The cost of full-stack web development depends on the features your product needs. A simple marketing website is not comparable to a multi-user application, an admin dashboard, or an online store. React, Next.js, and Python are implementation tools; the technology stack alone does not determine the project's price.

## A rough estimate for custom development

A small website or MVP may start at around **$5,000–$15,000**. An application with user accounts, a database, dashboards, and third-party integrations may cost **$15,000–$40,000 or more**. Online payments, multiple user roles, custom design, AI features, and special security requirements can increase both delivery time and budget.

These are planning estimates, not fixed market prices. Actual costs vary by location, developer experience, design quality, and project scope. To get comparable proposals, define essential features, key pages, user roles, and required integrations.

## Hiring a React, Next.js, or Python developer

If you need a **React or Next.js** developer, review responsive portfolio projects and ask how the interface will connect to APIs and backend services. For a **Python** backend, relevant experience includes API design, databases, testing, and deployment. If the project needs all of these areas, clarify ownership and coordination from the start.

Before work begins, ask:

- What features and files are included in the final delivery?
- Are design, testing, and deployment included in the estimate?
- Who will own the code and service accounts?
- How will post-launch support and bug fixes work?
- How will a change in scope affect the timeline and budget?

## Freelancer or agency?

A freelancer can be a good fit for a focused project that benefits from direct communication. An agency may provide access to multiple specialties—such as design, frontend, and backend development—but its process and pricing can differ. Choose based on product complexity, the range of expertise required, your budget, and your preferred way of working.

Whichever model you choose, put the scope, schedule, payment milestones, acceptance criteria, and maintenance costs in writing. Ask the developer to explain important technical trade-offs as well.

## Final thoughts

For a useful estimate, define a realistic first version of the product and its essential features. Then compare proposals based on the same scope and deliverables. Clear agreements and code ownership matter just as much as the technology and price.
