The cost of AI development depends on what you actually need to build. Adding an API-powered assistant to a website is very different from training a custom machine-learning model or securely connecting AI to a company's internal data. Before comparing quotes, define the problem, users, and expected outcome.

## A rough cost estimate

Freelance rates vary considerably across markets. As an initial international benchmark, you may see rates around **$30–$70 per hour** for less experienced developers and **$100–$200 or more** for established specialists. These are not fixed market rates; location, expertise, and engagement model all affect pricing.

A narrowly scoped feature using existing AI services may cost a few thousand dollars. A complete MVP—with a user interface, data integration, output evaluation, and deployment—could cost around **$10,000–$50,000 or more**. Any estimate made without a defined scope is only an early approximation.

## What affects the price?

- **Customization:** Using an existing AI API is usually simpler than training or fine-tuning a custom model.
- **Data readiness:** Scattered, incomplete, or unclean data takes extra time to prepare.
- **Integrations:** Connecting to databases, a CRM, or internal software adds complexity.
- **Security and privacy:** Access controls and sensitive-data handling should be part of the design from the start.
- **Testing and maintenance:** Quality evaluation, monitoring, and post-launch improvements also contribute to the total cost.

## How to choose the right developer

Look beyond an “AI expert” title or a list of libraries. Review relevant work and ask about the developer's experience shipping production software. Discuss how they will measure output quality, handle uncertain responses, and communicate the limitations of their proposed approach. For machine-learning features built with **Python**, data and deployment experience matter just as much as algorithm knowledge.

A solid proposal should define scope, deliverables, payment milestones, timeline, acceptance criteria, and exclusions. Clarify who owns the code and service accounts, and how post-launch support will be priced.

## Final thoughts

To compare proposals fairly, give several developers the same project brief. The lowest quote is not always the least expensive option in the long run; data quality, testing, and maintenance can make a meaningful difference. Start with a small, measurable feature and expand the scope based on what you learn.
